What Artificial Turf Installation Involves

Good installation is mostly about what happens before the turf ever gets rolled out. We remove the existing lawn or dirt, grade the area for drainage, and compact a crushed rock base so the surface doesn't shift or develop low spots over time. A weed barrier goes down next, then the turf itself, cut and seamed so the pattern lines up and joints aren't visible.

We finish by adding infill, which keeps the blades standing upright and adds a bit of cushioning, and securing the perimeter with edging so it stays put along walkways and planting beds. Done right, it should be hard to tell it isn't a real lawn from a few feet away.

When You Need Artificial Turf

Artificial turf tends to make the most sense for side yards and small lots where grass never gets enough sun or space to grow well, high-traffic areas where kids or pets wear down real grass faster than it can recover, and anywhere you're tired of the weekly mowing and watering cycle. It's also a common choice for homeowners converting a lawn for water savings but who still want the visual green of grass rather than gravel or ground cover.

Why Real Grass Struggles Here

Most lawn grass varieties planted decades ago in this area were never suited to hot, dry summers. They need frequent watering just to survive, and even then they thin out, go patchy, or brown off in the heat. Shade from mature trees, foot traffic from kids and pets, and compacted soil all make it worse.

Artificial turf sidesteps the problem entirely since it doesn't need water, sunlight, or soil quality to look good. It just needs a properly built base underneath it, which is the part most homeowners underestimate when they consider a DIY install.

What Affects the Cost

Artificial turf pricing depends on a few things:

  • The total square footage being covered
  • The turf product you choose, since pile height, density, and backing quality all vary
  • How much old lawn, concrete, or debris needs to be removed first
  • Site conditions like grading and drainage, which affect how much base prep is needed
  • Whether you want pet-specific infill for odor control

We'll walk your property and give you a written estimate that accounts for your specific site, not a generic per-square-foot number that doesn't hold up once work starts.

New Installation vs. Replacing Old Turf

If you already have artificial turf that's gone flat, faded, or started to smell despite regular cleaning, it's usually the infill and base that have broken down rather than the material itself. In some cases we can flush and refresh the infill to extend its life. Once the backing has degraded or the base has shifted enough to create low spots, replacement is the more reliable fix, since patching an aging install rarely holds up as long as starting with a solid base again.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is artificial turf safe for pets?

Yes, and we install pet-friendly products with a permeable backing that lets liquid drain through instead of pooling. Pairing that with an odor-control infill and periodic rinsing keeps it manageable even with multiple dogs using the yard daily.

How hot does artificial turf get in the sun?

It can get warmer than real grass on direct-sun summer afternoons, similar to how a dark driveway heats up. Lighter-colored turf products and infill options that reflect more heat help, and a quick rinse with a hose cools it down fast if it's an issue for bare feet or pets.

How long does artificial turf last?

Most quality turf products last fifteen to twenty years with normal use, though heavy pet traffic or poor base prep can shorten that. Proper installation matters more than the product itself when it comes to how long it actually holds up.

Does artificial turf need any maintenance at all?

Much less than real grass, but not zero. Occasional rinsing, brushing the blades upright in high-traffic spots, and clearing leaves or debris keeps it looking good. There's no mowing, fertilizing, or watering involved.

Can artificial turf be installed over existing concrete or pavers?

In some cases, yes, with the right base preparation and drainage plan. We'll assess your specific surface during the estimate and let you know whether it's a good candidate or whether removal makes more sense.
