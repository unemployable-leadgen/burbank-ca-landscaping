What Sod Installation Involves

The finished lawn is only as good as the ground underneath it, so we start there: removing old grass or debris, grading the area so water drains the right direction, and working in soil amendments if the existing dirt is compacted or low in nutrients. We lay the sod in a brick-like pattern with tight seams so it knits together instead of showing gaps as it settles.

Once it's down, we water it in thoroughly on day one and walk you through a watering schedule for the first few weeks while the roots establish into the soil below.

When You Need Sod Installation

Sod makes sense when you want a finished lawn fast rather than waiting weeks for seed to germinate and fill in, when you're dealing with large bare or dead patches that would take seed a long time to recover, for new construction where there's no lawn at all yet, or when a previous lawn died back so badly that patching it isn't realistic anymore. It's also a common choice before selling a house, since a full green lawn shows far better than seed that hasn't filled in yet.

Why Lawns Go Patchy or Die Out

Uneven watering is the most common cause, since some parts of a yard naturally get more sun or drainage than others and a fixed sprinkler schedule doesn't account for that. Compacted soil is another big one: it keeps water and roots from getting past the surface, so grass thins out even when it's getting watered. Heavy foot traffic wears grass down faster than it can recover, and heat stress during a long, dry summer finishes off whatever was already struggling.

Fresh sod on properly prepared soil gives the lawn a real reset instead of asking struggling grass to somehow recover on its own.

What Affects the Cost

Sod installation pricing depends on a few factors:

  • The total square footage being covered
  • The grass variety you choose, since some types cost more than others
  • How much soil prep the site needs, including grading and amendments
  • Whether old grass, weeds, or debris need to be removed first

We'll give you a clear written estimate based on your actual yard, not a rough per-pallet number that doesn't account for site conditions.

Sod vs. Artificial Turf

Sod is the right call if you want a real lawn and are willing to keep up with watering and mowing, or if you're in an area with enough irrigation flexibility to support it. Artificial turf makes more sense if you'd rather eliminate watering and mowing entirely, especially on smaller lots or high-traffic areas where real grass struggles to keep up. We install both and will give you an honest read on which one actually fits how you use your yard, rather than steering you toward whichever job is bigger.

Frequently Asked Questions

How soon can I walk on new sod?

Light foot traffic is usually fine after about two weeks, once the roots have started knitting into the soil below. We'll give you specific guidance based on the grass variety and time of year, since establishment time varies a bit with temperature.

How much watering does new sod need?

New sod needs frequent, light watering for the first couple of weeks to keep the roots from drying out while they establish, then we transition you to a normal deep-watering schedule. We'll walk you through exactly what to do so the lawn doesn't dry out or get overwatered.

Can sod be installed over an existing dead lawn?

Yes, but the old grass and thatch need to come out first and the soil needs proper prep, otherwise it won't root well into what's underneath. We handle that removal and prep as part of the installation.

What sod varieties work best in our climate?

Warm-season grass varieties generally hold up better to our hot, dry summers than cool-season types, though the right choice depends on how much sun the area gets and how the lawn will be used. We'll recommend a variety suited to your specific yard.

How long until sod looks fully established?

It looks green and full almost immediately, but full root establishment takes several weeks to a couple of months. Avoid heavy use or mowing too short during that window so the roots have time to take hold.
